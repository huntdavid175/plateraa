# Product Marketing Context

**Document version:** v1
**Last updated:** 2026-10-15

## Product Overview

**One-liner:** Plateraa runs a small food business from one tablet: every order in one place, every cedi counted, and a clear view of profit.

**What it does:** An offline-first counter app on the vendor's own Android tablet takes walk-in, phone and online orders, tracks the cash drawer and stock, and keeps working without internet. Each vendor gets an online store link to share on WhatsApp and Instagram, where customers order and pay by mobile money straight into the vendor's own Moolre account. A web dashboard shows the owner today's sales, expenses and profit from anywhere.

**Product category:** Point of sale (POS) / restaurant management for small food vendors; also "online ordering for food vendors in Ghana".

**Product type:** SaaS (tablet app + web dashboard + hosted online store).

**Business model:** Free to start and free to use. Plateraa takes 2% only on money a customer pays online through Plateraa (payment links and the online store). Cash sales are free. Moolre's own transaction fee is separate. (User decision, 15 Oct 2026; replaces the "prepaid 30-day pass" plan in plan.md R2 for marketing purposes.)

## Target Audience

**Target companies:** Small, mostly informal food businesses in Ghana (Accra first): chop bars, fast food spots, cafés and bakeries, juice bars, cloud kitchens, food trucks. Usually 1–10 staff, mostly cash, often tracked in a notebook.

**Decision-makers:** The owner (often also cooking or at the counter); sometimes a manager they trust.

**Primary use case:** Know exactly what was sold, what came in and what's left, without a notebook, while taking orders from walk-ins, calls and WhatsApp/Instagram.

**Jobs to be done:**

- Take every order quickly, from every channel, without losing any.
- Know at the end of the day that the money is right, and whether the day made a profit.
- Let customers order and pay online without paying a delivery app's commission.

**Use cases:**

- Lunch rush at a chop bar: staff ring up orders on the tablet, the kitchen sees what to cook, the drawer is counted at close.
- A customer on Instagram taps the store link, orders jollof for pickup and pays by MoMo; the tablet chimes.
- The owner, away from the shop, checks today's sales and expenses on their phone.
- Something runs out: one tap marks it sold out everywhere, including the online store.

## Problems & Pain Points

**Core problem:** Orders arrive from the counter, phone calls, WhatsApp and Instagram, and the money is tracked in a notebook (if at all). The owner can't tell what really came in, where it went, or whether the business made money.

**Why alternatives fall short:**

- Notebooks: slow, get lost, can't add up, can't be checked from home.
- Big POS systems: built for restaurants abroad, priced in dollars, need card hardware and strong internet.
- Delivery apps (Bolt Food, Chowdeck): take a large commission per order and own the customer.
- WhatsApp ordering by hand: messages get missed, payment is chased by screenshot.

**What it costs them:** Lost and wrong orders, missing cash, food that runs out or goes to waste, commission paid to delivery apps, and hours at night doing the books.

**Emotional tension:** Not knowing whether staff are honest with the money; feeling busy but not seeing profit; worry about tech that breaks when the internet drops.

## Competitive Landscape

**Direct:** Generic POS apps (e.g. Loyverse, Square-style tills) — not built for Ghanaian mobile-money, mixed-channel food vendors; often need constant internet or card hardware.
**Secondary:** Delivery platforms (Bolt Food, Chowdeck) — bring orders but take a big commission per order and keep the customer relationship.
**Indirect:** The notebook and a calculator; WhatsApp chats and MoMo screenshots.

## Differentiation

**Key differentiators:**

- Works offline: orders keep going when the Wi-Fi or hotspot drops and sync later.
- Runs on the vendor's own budget Android tablet; no special hardware to buy.
- Mobile money goes straight into the vendor's own Moolre account; Plateraa never holds their money.
- One store link for WhatsApp and Instagram, with no commission per order beyond 2% on online payments.
- One inbox for counter, phone and online orders.
- Built in Ghana, for cedis, MoMo and how food vendors actually work.

**How we do it differently:** Cash-first and mobile-money-native, offline-first, and free until the vendor gets paid online.

**Why that's better:** Nothing to pay up front, nothing to lose when the internet drops, and the money is always the vendor's.

**Why customers choose us:** It's free to start, it's simple enough for any staff member, and it shows the owner where the money went.

## Objections

| Objection                            | Response                                                                                               |
| ------------------------------------ | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| "It will cost me money every month." | No monthly fee. Cash sales are free. You pay 2% only when a customer pays you online through Plateraa. |
| "Our internet is not reliable."      | The tablet keeps taking orders offline and catches up when the connection is back.                     |
| "My staff won't manage it."          | Each staff member has a PIN; order entry is a few taps; we help set it up.                             |
| "Will you hold my money?"            | No. Mobile money goes straight into your own Moolre account. We never touch it.                        |
| "I need to buy expensive equipment." | It runs on an ordinary Android tablet (8–10"). A receipt printer is optional.                          |
| "Do I need a Moolre account?"        | Yes, to take mobile money online. It's your own merchant account; we help you connect it during setup. |

**Anti-persona:** Large multi-branch restaurant chains needing card terminals and table service floor plans; businesses outside Ghana; vendors unwilling to open their own Moolre account.

## Switching Dynamics

**Push:** The notebook doesn't add up; cash goes missing; orders get lost between WhatsApp and the counter; delivery apps take too much.
**Pull:** Free to start; see profit clearly; a store link customers can pay through; works offline.
**Habit:** "The notebook works fine"; staff used to the old way; fear of learning tech during a rush.
**Anxiety:** Cost, hidden fees, losing data, the internet failing mid-service, who holds the money.
